## AI assistance and truthful attribution

You specified the app and directed its development, and AI assisted with its
implementation. You can describe it as your project without saying you manually
wrote every line. OpenAI's individual-service terms assign its rights, if any,
in output to the user, to the extent allowed by law. They also say output may
not be unique and exclude rights in third-party output. This is a contractual
allocation, not a guarantee of copyright or exclusivity. [OpenAI Terms of Use](https://openai.com/policies/row-terms-of-use/)

AI copyright treatment varies. For example, the U.S. Copyright Office requires
human authorship for U.S. protection and evaluates human contributions to mixed
works. Do not assume that a name in LICENSE.txt makes every generated line
exclusively protectable worldwide. Keep your specifications, design choices,
review notes, edits, and dated Git history. [U.S. Copyright Office report](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)

## Keeping control

- You chose public visibility. A public GitHub repository permits viewing and
  forking under GitHub's terms, even if
  you do not choose an open-source license. [GitHub licensing guidance](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository)
- Use the proprietary license for new versions where you hold the relevant
  rights. Version 1.0 contained an MIT license: recipients who obtained valid
  rights under that version keep them. A new notice does not retroactively
  withdraw those grants. Retain that release privately for provenance.
- Obtain suitable written IP assignments and confidentiality agreements before
  outside developers contribute. Check any employer, university, or other
  applicable agreements; ownership cannot be established by a README alone.
- Keep source backups and dependency provenance. Private access reduces exposure;
  it is not a legal guarantee that nobody can copy or independently recreate an app.

## Third-party code and distribution

You do not acquire ownership of Python, Qt/PySide, FFmpeg, or other dependencies.
Their license rights survive your proprietary app license. In particular,
LGPL-covered runtime libraries must remain replaceable, and distribution carries
notice and corresponding-source requirements. Review
[DISTRIBUTION.md](DISTRIBUTION.md) before sharing binaries outside your private
workspace/account. [Qt's LGPL obligations](https://www.qt.io/development/open-source-lgpl-obligations)

Before commercial distribution, have a qualified IP/software-licensing lawyer
review the ownership chain, AI contributions, dependency compliance, any codec
patent obligations, and customer terms. The current setup preserves your control
over the project without claiming ownership of other people's work.
